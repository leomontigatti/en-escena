# Judging

Rules for presentations, judging, program, results, scores and feedback.

## Participation And Judging

- `Presentación` is one-to-one with a choreography and has a unique order number within the event. It is a row of its own that holds the order number and nothing else about the choreography: no copy of its schedule, category or group type, so everything shown beside the number is the choreography's current data.
- A presentation is created in two ways only: by `Ordenar automáticamente`, and, once the event has at least one presentation, by placing a late choreography by hand. It is never a side effect of a payment, an allocation or a registration.
- Getting an order number requires nothing of the choreography's money: one that is `Seña pendiente`, derived on read as `docs/domain/finances.md` defines it, is numbered like any other, even if it is operationally incomplete. Nothing else is required either: a choreography always has a category and a schedule (`docs/domain/choreographies.md`).
- Keeping an order number has no condition either. A choreography below `Señada` stays in the administrative list and in the portal, numbered or not, and in the public program once numbered and its day published; the first two mark it `Seña pendiente`, the program does not.
- The automatic ordering sorts in blocks: schedule (date, time, then name), then experience level in the order `nudo`, `amateur`, `profesional`, `pre_elite`, `elite`, `pro_am`, with a choreography that declares no level last (a grupal with `Sin nivel` included), then category age order (minimum age, maximum age, then name), then group type in the order solo, duo, trio, grupal. The level order is a fixed rule of the domain, not an event setting, and a schedule whose choreographies declare no level is ordered as if the key were not there. Modality and submodality play no part; a schedule already restricts the modalities it accepts, and optionally the categories too.
- Inside a block the order follows the choreography number, corrected by `Separación de bailarines`: two presentations that share an active dancer need at least four others between them, counted within one schedule. The ordering places rows one at a time and takes the first remaining choreography, by number, that shares no dancer with the previous four positions; when every remaining one conflicts, it places the lowest number and the clash is flagged. It never breaks the block sequence to satisfy the gap. Only dancers count, never professors, with the same rule for every group type; the four is fixed, not an event setting.
- `Presentación fija`: once a schedule has any presentation with a score or a disqualification, every numbered presentation of that schedule is frozen, the unscored tail included. A schedule that has started has been announced and printed, so its lineup stays put. Frozen is derived on read, never stored, and a late choreography placed into such a schedule becomes frozen the moment it is numbered.
- A frozen number is immutable: neither the automatic ordering nor a manual move changes it, and no move may take it as a target. The other numbers are handed out over the free positions, in ascending order: every position from 1 that is not a frozen number and not a gap between two consecutive frozen presentations, of one schedule or of two. A gap inside or between schedules that already ran stays a gap; the gaps outside are closed as before.
- `Ordenar automáticamente` first asks which days to order, none meaning the whole event (an event of one day skips the question), then always asks for confirmation, naming those days and whether some of their presentations stay in place. It orders everything of those days that is not frozen from scratch, discarding every manual change among those rows. The numbers stay one sequence across the event: every other day keeps its numbered rows in their current order and only shifts with the days before it, while its late choreographies wait for their own day to be ordered. It renumbers existing presentations in place, so their judge assignments survive, adds every choreography of those days that has no number yet, and deletes nothing. It is refused only when nothing is left to order. Its confirmation reports what it ordered and what it left in place.
- A late choreography of a schedule that already ran is ordered after that schedule's frozen run when the next position is free, and after the next frozen schedule when it is not; it is never inserted between two frozen numbers. Until it is numbered it carries the `Cronograma evaluado` warning, which says it will be placed after the schedules that already ran. The dancer gap is counted against the frozen tail as well as against the rows being placed.
- The administrator changes the order by dragging a row or typing its number. Both persist immediately, without confirmation, permute the free positions only, and leave those numbers contiguous. Neither is available before the first automatic ordering. A frozen row has no grip and its number is locked; dropping onto a frozen row, or typing a frozen number, is refused. A late choreography takes its number the same way, which creates its presentation and shifts the later unfrozen ones down.
- A row may be moved across blocks: the choreography's schedule stays the capacity truth, and the order is the administrator's.
- `Advertencia` is derived on read, never stored, and blocks nothing: not the ordering, not a judge assignment, not publishing the program. Its kinds are `Seña pendiente`, `Cronograma evaluado` (a choreography without a number whose schedule already has a frozen presentation), `Separación` (both presentations of a clash, each naming the other's number and the shared dancer), `Fuera de bloque` (the smallest set of presentations that, taken out, leaves the rest in block order) `Nivel sin asignar` (a choreography whose experience level is required and missing, numbered or not — the ordering sorts it last and only administration can repair it; a grupal, for which the level is optional, raises none when it declares no level) and `Sin jueces` (a numbered presentation, not disqualified, with no judge assigned while another presentation of its schedule has one: a schedule nobody has assigned yet is not flagged, so it catches the presentation numbered after its schedule's panel was assigned). `Separación`, `Fuera de bloque`, `Nivel sin asignar` and `Sin jueces` are shown to administration only; the portal shows `Seña pendiente` and nothing else, the public program shows none.
- The list's `Estado` filter keeps the rows whose badge reads the answer chosen: `Con advertencias`, `Evaluada`, `Descalificada` or `Retirada`. An evaluated row is never `Con advertencias`, since its badge replaces the warnings. The `Presentaciones con advertencias` notice counts the day's `Con advertencias` rows, and its `Ver` applies that answer; once applied the notice offers nothing, and the filter's own control takes it off.
- A numbered choreography stays correctable until it is evaluated (`docs/domain/choreographies.md`, "Choreography Locks"). The correction keeps the number and surfaces as `Fuera de bloque` when it changes the category, the schedule or the group type, or as `Separación` when it changes the dancers.
- Deleting a numbered choreography before it is evaluated deletes its presentation and its judge assignments. The number is left as a gap until the next move or automatic ordering closes it.
- Withdrawing a numbered choreography before it is evaluated keeps its presentation and its judge assignments (`docs/domain/choreographies.md`, "Removing a choreography"). Its number is held like a frozen one, in any schedule: neither the automatic ordering nor a manual move changes it, it has no grip and its number is locked, and no move may take it as a target (`Esa presentación está retirada: conserva su número.`, `Esa posición es de una presentación retirada.`). Unlike frozen numbers, two withdrawn ones do not close off the gap between them. Nobody dances in a withdrawn slot, so the ordering does not count it as rest for a shared dancer, exactly as `Separación` does not. A choreography withdrawn before it was numbered is never numbered, and the list shows it only under `Estado: Retirada`.
- A withdrawn presentation reads `Retirada` in place of every other badge, raises no `Advertencia` and counts for no other row's: not the dancer gap, not the block order, not `Sin jueces`. It is read-only for judges too: it keeps the judges it had, and the judge edit neither adds nor removes any, nor lets its row be chosen. It is never scored or disqualified: a judge's save is refused (`La coreografía fue retirada, no se pueden guardar puntajes.`), and administration's scores view says it is withdrawn and offers no disqualification, which the server refuses too. It has no result and is not on the `Resultados` list.
- Presentations with scores or disqualification are not reorderable, and neither is any other numbered presentation of their schedule (`Presentación fija` above).
- Only administration disqualifies a presentation, and only administration reinstates it, from the presentation's scores view at any time, with no window and no reason, so a disqualification can be settled long after the judges' day has closed. Judges never do: their forms offer neither.
- A disqualification closes the presentation for the whole panel, takes it out of the results and shows as `Descalificada` in every assigned judge's list; the scores already saved are kept, and a judge may still leave a `Devolución` on it so the academy hears why. Reinstating brings those scores back exactly as they were.
- `Estado de participación` values include `sin presentación`, `pendiente`, `evaluada`, `descalificada` and `ausente inferida`. `Descalificada` means disqualified, `evaluada` means evaluated and not disqualified, and the administrative list shows those two as badges, each replacing the row's `Advertencia` badge, and nothing for the rest.
- `Asignación de juez` relates one judge to one presentation, so a choreography without a number cannot be assigned. Any internal user with the `judge` role who is not suspended can be assigned; the role is global, with no per-event membership.
- Judges are assigned to, and removed from, many presentations at once. Reassigning same judge to same presentation must not create duplicates.
- An assignment survives the judge's suspension or a change of role, a new automatic ordering and any manual move. Nothing marks it and nothing removes it automatically.
- Assigning a judge creates the assignment only. The score row is created on the judge's first save, so an assignment with no score is a judge who has not scored yet.
- Removing a judge assignment is refused once that judge has a score row for that presentation, so no score is ever orphaned. A bulk removal removes the rest and reports how many it kept.

- The bulk judge dialog starts each judge's checkbox from where the judge stands across the chosen rows: checked on all of them, unchecked on none, mixed on some. A judge left as it started is not submitted, so a judge on some rows stays on exactly those; a click on a mixed judge goes through mixed, so the administrator can always return to the starting state. A judge who is no longer assignable can only be taken off.
- The judge edit's messages count what it reached, not what was asked: an assignment already in place and a judge nobody had are not counted. A removal that would take apart only scored pairs is refused rather than reported as a success, and an edit that both assigns and removes says both.
- The `Ordenar automáticamente` dialog closes both of its steps when the ordering is confirmed, since the result is announced by the list's toast. Backing out of the confirmation keeps the chosen days, and leaving the first step asks nothing.
- The automatic ordering is offered on every day with a row that is not withdrawn, whatever the `Estado` filter shows.
- The running order is the list's only sort, and rows without a number always close the list, in either direction.
- A row already numbered can take any free place in the order, never a held one (a frozen or withdrawn number is refused); a late row can take any free number up to one past the last.
- A drag means "put this row where that one is": the number moved to is the number of the row it is dropped on, and the rows on screen reorder the same way. A drop onto a held row or a held place is ignored rather than shown and then undone.
- A manual move names the number the row had when it was picked up. When that number has changed since, the move is refused and nothing is written, so two administrators moving at once never overwrite each other. A move that works shows no message, since the refreshed list is the answer.
- The warnings rank: `Sin jueces` comes first and is the only one drawn as destructive, since a presentation nobody judges cannot be scored. The rest follow `Seña pendiente`, `Cronograma evaluado`, `Separación`, `Fuera de bloque` and `Nivel sin asignar`, and the same order picks the row's badge.
- A presentation's panel is listed by the judge's name.
- A disqualification records when it happened, not who made it.
- The music download starts from its dialog without leaving the list. The dialog turns down a day with no music before any download is asked for, since a failed download has no page to say so.

## Program And Results

- Program can be published before results and reflects current order; it does not freeze a copy.
- Program is published one day at a time, a day being the date its schedules share. Every day starts hidden, a day that gains its first schedule later included, and is visible only once administration publishes it: a day not published may still be reordered, by itself or with the event, so its numbers are not final and reach neither the academies nor the public. A schedule added to a published day is published with it.
- Administration chooses the visible days from the presentations list's actions menu, for the active event: `Mostrar/ocultar programa` opens a dialog listing each day with a numbered presentation, and each day visible now so it can always be hidden, with a checkbox per day; `Guardar` sets the whole selection at once.
- Program is public, without login, at `/programa`. It shows the active event only, and only its published days: their tabs, rows, award ceremonies and printed pages. With no day published, or with no active event, the page says that no program is published and does not reveal which of the two it is.
- Program lists every presentation of the published days in order, with no gaps, and shows non-competitive data only. A withdrawn presentation is listed at its number with a minus beside it (read `Retirada` by a screen reader and in its tooltip; on a phone, its number in a muted badge with a minus), so the numbering never shows an unexplained gap; the printed program writes `Retirada` after its name and the spreadsheet export marks it in an `Estado` column. The check of an evaluated presentation sits beside its number the same way, with no column of its own. The program shows nothing else that is competitive: it hides scores, averages, awards, disqualifications, inferred absences and every `Advertencia`. It names the dancers of a solo and of a duo, and of no other group type. It announces each schedule's `awardCeremony` when one is set: on screen on that schedule's day, on paper after the schedule's last presentation.
- On screen, the program follows the day being danced, the judging day (to 03:00 the next morning). On that day's tab, an evaluated presentation (any score or a disqualification) is marked `Ya se presentó` for the whole day; a disqualified one reads exactly like a scored one. The tab reads `En vivo` from the start of the day's first schedule until its last presentation still taking part (not withdrawn) is evaluated, or 03:00, and while it does, the open tab reloads every minute. Paper and the portal carry neither. The program opens on that tab rather than on its first day when loaded while it reads `En vivo`, at the page of its last evaluated presentation. No list of the order has a tab for every day: each opens on its first.
- Program has its own print layout, one run of pages per schedule. The academy's list of its own presentations on the portal does not print.
- The portal lists the academy's own choreographies with the same published numbers as the program: numbered ones in order, and the ones not placed yet as `Sin número`. A withdrawn one is listed only while it shows a published number, marked as on the program, and never as owing its deposit. A choreography on a day not published has its number withheld on the server and reads exactly like one not placed yet, sorted among them, with no mark or explanation; while no day with a presentation is published, the academy sees the same empty state as before the event is ordered. It is read-only and links to the public program once any day is published.
- Results are not a ranking. There are no positions, no ties, no competitive grouping and no public results page: `Cupo de cronograma` never defines a competitive group, and nothing about results is readable without login.
- What a presentation earns is its `Premio`, read off its own rounded average in the bands fixed by the domain ("Scores And Feedback" below). Two presentations that average the same take the same award, and no presentation is compared to another.
- Administration reads the active event's results on a list of its own, `Resultados`: every numbered presentation in running order with its live `Premio` and average, a presentation the panel has not reached left blank, and an evaluated one the academies do not see yet marked `Sin publicar`.
- Publishing results is a snapshot of the active event taken by administration from the `Resultados` list's actions menu, with no precondition: not a resolved group, not the event's temporal state. `Mostrar resultados` publishes every presentation evaluated at that moment, `Actualizar resultados` adds the ones evaluated since, and `Ocultar resultados` takes everything down at once. Publishing again after hiding starts from what is evaluated then.
- The snapshot stores only membership: the event's publication timestamp and, per presentation, the moment it entered the snapshot. A presentation is published when its event is published and it is in the snapshot. Nothing about the result itself is frozen, so a correction to a published presentation —an administrative edit, a disqualification or a reinstatement— reaches the academy immediately, with no need to publish again.
- Administration reads, while results are published, how many presentations the academies see, when they were published, and how many are evaluated and still waiting to be added, and only administration publishes, updates or hides. `Resultados` and a presentation's scores view are not among the auditor's sections (docs/domain/access.md): the auditor does not check the panel's work.
- An academy reads its results in the portal, behind its login and only for its own published presentations: the award, the average and, per judge, the judge's name, their score, the sheet breakdown when there is one, and their `Devolución`. Nothing about results appears before administration publishes, and a presentation that is not the academy's own, is not published, or belongs to hidden results answers "not found".
- Hidden from the academy are judges who never scored, who are left out entirely. A disqualified presentation shows as `Descalificada`, with no award, no average and no scores, and keeps every judge's `Devolución`.
- A `Particular` whose link is `Vinculado` reads, in `Coreografías`, the same published result for each active-event choreography its link reaches, in any academy and behind the same publication gate: the award, the average and each judge's name and score, and on a disqualified presentation only `Descalificada`. The sheet breakdown and the `Devolución` stay the academy's (ADR-0019).
- A published presentation that is not disqualified always has an award, so a `certificate` has one sentence: it certifies the dancer took part and names the award and the choreography. Every dancer still inscribed gets one, named as the roster names them; a disqualified presentation, a withdrawn choreography, a professor and an edition with no design get none. The academy opens them from the evaluation's `Acciones` (`Descargar certificados`: one dancer's, or all of them in one PDF with a page each), and a `Vinculado` `Particular` opens its own from `Coreografías`. Each opens as a PDF in a new tab, for the active event only, behind the same gate as the result.
- Administration downloads the program as an Excel spreadsheet from the presentations list, and the results from the `Resultados` list, each for the whole event or one day, in running order. Both sheets name the dancers of a solo, a duo and a trio, one per line, and count a larger group's; the results' sheet adds each presentation's average and `Premio`; it reads live results, whether or not they are published, and lists only presentations with a result: one not evaluated yet, with nothing left to average, or disqualified is left out.

- Certificates list their recipients by last name, then first name, the order the academy reads them. A certificate's sentence wraps at spaces inside the design's frame and shrinks as a whole when it does not fit, so a long name is never smaller than the words around it.
- The results export offers only the days that have a scored presentation.
- Publishing results is refused when the active event changed after its confirmation opened, since another administrator may have switched events. The moment results were published is shown in business time, since results are announced from the venue.
- The program-visibility save carries the event the dialog was opened for, and is refused after a switch of the active event rather than publishing a program nobody saw. Its confirmation names the days left visible, or says that the whole program is hidden.
- Ordering and program visibility both refuse a day outside the event's schedules, since it would publish or order nothing a schedule holds.
- The program export is offered only for the days that have a numbered row.
- The program spreadsheet has one row per presentation in running order. Category is kept apart from group type and modality apart from submodality, and the day is a real date so each can be filtered alone. It lists every professor of a choreography, whatever its group, by surname then name.
- The administration's spreadsheet shows the academy's province, which is blank for an academy registered before the province was asked.
- A schedule's award ceremony has both a date and a time, or neither. Only the public program announces it. A ceremony after midnight falls on the next day, and its line names that day when it is not the schedule's own.
- A schedule that nobody presents in has no page in the printed program. Every row of a printed page is one height, whatever its two lines hold.
- A choreography's music download carries its order number only where the reader may see it: always for administration, and for the academy once its day's program is published.
- The public program reads the session the request carries and nothing more: it never requires one, redirects or writes a cookie, and the reader's session stays out of the cache. Reading the session can still extend its expiry in the database, as any session read does.
- The participation list is one design for the portal and the public program. The public program adds the academy column and the portal the `Nivel` column, which tells apart presentations that otherwise differ in nothing else.
- The day tab only narrows what is on screen, since the whole list is already loaded. The chosen day stays in the URL, so a reload or a shared link lands on it.
- The live-day page records the judging day it knew, so a page left open when the next judging day begins asks for the new day's data. A day whose presentations are all withdrawn is over at once, since nothing is left to dance.
- A page loaded on the live day opens once at its show page, on the URL it was loaded with: a link naming a day, a page or a search stays where it points. When that page is past the first, it is written into the URL with its day, so a reload after the show still lands on it.
- An empty level reads as the reason it is empty (`Sin asignar`, `Sin nivel` or `No aplica`). The portal and the spreadsheets show it; the public program hides it.
- A choreography's own number is distinct from its order number, and the program shows it only in a linked name's hover title; the public program, whose names link nowhere, does not show it.
- The portal's presentations list says above the list how many choreographies still owe their deposit, linking to `Finanzas`; no row says it, and the public program never does.
- An individual's choreography with an unpublished result reads `Sin publicar`, never as an empty award.
- On the academy's presentations list, a published presentation's name opens what the judges said about it, and every other name opens its choreography. The academy's results list opens that evaluation from every name, and shows only the academy's own published rows, with no academy column, and a card per row on a phone.

## Scores And Feedback

- A `Puntaje` belongs to one judge assignment for one presentation, and its row is created on the judge's first save. Nothing creates it empty, and administration never creates one on a judge's behalf: the answer to a judge who never scored is to remove the assignment.
- A score value is between 0 and 100 and moves in steps of 0.5, which is what the panel works in. The same applies to a criterion value, between 0 and its own maximum. Both are enforced in validation and as check constraints.
- Every score, sheet total and average the app writes uses a decimal point, on every surface: the judge's screens, administration and the academy portal. It is a deliberate exception to es-AR formatting, and it covers scores only — money and dates keep their es-AR form. The results spreadsheet is the one exception: it stores the average as a number with two decimals so it sorts and filters, and the spreadsheet app shows it with the separator of its own locale. The number input accepts a point and nothing else, and a score has to read the same on the tablet that typed it and on the page that reviews it.
- A presentation is scored with a single 0-100 value, unless its submodality has criteria, in which case it is scored on a sheet (`Planilla`) of one field per criterion. A submodality with no criteria, and a modality with no submodalities, score with a single value. So does a grupal with `Sin nivel`, even when its submodality has criteria: it chose not to compete by level, so it is not put on a sheet built for levels. The judge's form, the judge's list, the save and administration's edit read the sheet through this one decision, so a stored score always matches its sheet; a choreography is locked for correction once evaluated, so its sheet cannot switch after the first score.
- A `Criterio` belongs to one submodality and has a name, a maximum that is a whole number from 1, and a kind that either adds or deducts. A criterion with no experience level is general (`Evaluación general`) and is on every sheet of the submodality; one with a level is that level's `Técnico obligatorio` and is only on the sheets of choreographies at that level. A sheet is the general criteria followed by those of the choreography's level, and a choreography with no level that is not a `Sin nivel` grupal —its level is `No aplica`, or `Sin asignar`— is scored on the general criteria alone. A name is unique within its sheet.
- On every complete sheet the adding maxima total exactly 100, so a sheet can always reach 100: for every level some category of the modality offers, the general adding maxima plus that level's total 100, and where no level applies the general ones total 100 alone. Deduction maxima sit outside that total, because a deduction is a penalty and not a share of the score.
- A submodality's criteria are edited and saved one sheet at a time, each as a whole: the general criteria, or one level's own. A level's own are only saved when they complete the general ones to exactly 100. The general criteria are saved at up to 100 —exactly 100 where a category of the modality has no levels and scores on them alone— and the sheets they leave short are listed as incomplete (`Planilla incompleta`) until each is completed. An incomplete sheet cannot be scored: the judge's form says so and the save is refused, because the first score would lock the criteria with the sheet still short. A name the other part of its sheet already uses is refused.
- A submodality's criteria are locked once any presentation of that submodality has a score, so scores already given keep meaning the same thing.
- A sheet's total is the additions minus the deductions, clamped to 0 and 100. It is stored as the score's single value and recomputed on every save and on every administrative edit, so an average never re-derives a sheet.
- `Jornada` is the business date of three hours before now: a show runs past midnight, so the judges' day starts with its date and closes at 03:00 the next morning. It is computed on read, with no stored flag and no job.
- A presentation is open for its judges only while its choreography's schedule date is the current judging day: before that day there is nothing to score yet, and after 03:00 the next morning every judge write on it is refused. Within it a judge scores and corrects only their own work.
- A withdrawn presentation stays on its judges' lists at its number, reading `Retirada`: its name opens no form, its URL opens none, the `Pendientes` filter leaves it out, and saving the presentation before it moves on to the next one still owed, never to it.
- The judge's list and the write window are two rules. The list opens on the judging day, and reaches every other day of the active event the judge has presentations on, read-only: a past day to see what they scored, a coming one to see what they will face. On any day but the open one nothing opens the judge's form, and the write would be refused anyway. A judge sees only their own work on any day.
- Reopening a presentation a judge already scored opens their own score, their own sheet lines and their own `Devolución` in the form, so a correction is an edit and not a retype — and re-recording a `Devolución` on a scored presentation never asks for the number again. The list shows only the judge's own score, and only once it is complete, as that row's status; on a day that is not open, a score without a `Devolución` shows too, since it can no longer be fixed.
- Administration edits any score at any time, with no window, no reason asked and no trace kept. What it stores goes through the same validation as a judge's own save.
- There is no score annulment: every saved score counts. A score that is wrong is corrected, not set aside.
- A presentation is **evaluated** when it is disqualified or when any score row exists for it. That single fact is what locks a choreography for correction and deletion, freezes the numbers of its whole schedule against automatic ordering and manual moves, and guards judge-assignment removal.
- The average is the mean of the saved values of a non-disqualified presentation, rounded to two decimals. A presentation with no saved value has no average, and a disqualified one has none either.
- `Premio` is read off that rounded average, in bands fixed by the domain:

  | Rounded average | Award               |
  | --------------- | ------------------- |
  | below 60        | `Mención especial`  |
  | 60 to below 80  | `Medalla de bronce` |
  | 80 to below 90  | `Medalla de plata`  |
  | 90 or more      | `Medalla de oro`    |

  There are no positions, no ties and no competitive grouping in it: two presentations that average the same take the same award.

- `Devolución` is one optional private audio recorded by the judge for the academy, saved with the score that carries it and replaced or removed by the same save. It is allowed on a disqualified presentation, where it is stored with no value, so the academy still hears why.
- A judge's own status on a presentation is pending while there is no value, complete with a value and a `Devolución`, without feedback with a value and none, and disqualified whenever the presentation is. The list shows `Pendiente`, the judge's own score in place of a word when complete, `Sin devolución` as a warning — the `Devolución` is optional, but the judge should notice it is missing while the day is open — and `Descalificada`. On any other day the warning gives way to the score, and an unscored presentation is `Pendiente`, past and coming days alike. It is shown to that judge only, and it is not the presentation's `Estado de participación`, which answers for the whole panel.

- A judge's save is one upsert on their assignment, so a doubled tap cannot create two scores. On a disqualified presentation the stored value stays as it is, and only the feedback changes.
- A save reads the sheet's criteria from the database inside its transaction, not from the form: a criterion added since the page loaded must be filled, a removed one is never stored, and a field the form forgot is a refusal, not a zero.
- A sheet is saved as a whole: the stored values are cleared and the validated lines written in their place, so a removed criterion cannot survive under a total that ignores it.
- A score is stored with one decimal. The fields and the scores columns show it back without the trailing zero, so 90 reads as 90, and a field with nothing stored is empty rather than zero. Totals read the numbers.
- An administrator's correction saves one score, or one whole sheet, on its own. A judge's single score is saved alone, and a sheet is one save, because its lines and their total are one decision.
- A judge who has saved nothing has no feedback cell on the scores page, since an empty `Sin devolución` there would read as something forgotten.
- A save refused for a closed or not-started day says so: the day has closed, or has not started yet, and scores cannot be saved. A presentation whose choreography has no schedule yet is closed to judges, since there is no day for it to be open on.
- A criterion's name is compared trimmed, accent-insensitively and case-insensitively. The database index folds case only, so the save is stricter than the index: a name that differs from another by accent alone is refused by the save. An empty name is never a duplicate.
- A level's sheet stays listed while it holds criteria, even when no category offers that level any more. Its total counts the general criteria too, so its criteria can be cleared and its total still checked.
- On the sheet list a criterion's kind is its row's position: adds above the separator, deducts below. A level's counter shows the general criteria's share beside its own, and only the level's own share turns red, since only that part can be wrong.
- A modality's criteria stand as `none` (every submodality scores with one value), `incomplete` (some sheet misses 100) or `complete`. Its page, its list and the criteria editor count the same sheets.
- The `Profesional` badge sits beside a presentation's title only in the score dialog and sheet headings and on administration's scores page. The lists carry no mark.
- On the academy's portal, a sheet's breakdown shows what the dance earned, then what it lost, divided by a rule only when both sides have something. A deduction that bit carries a minus sign, and a zero reads as nothing taken away.

### Judge's screens

- The judge's list resumes from the last presentation the judge opened. That place is kept for the browser session on the device only, and it belongs to the show running now, so a day that is not open has none. A day in the URL that the judge has no presentations on falls back to the judging day.
- The judge's whole day is one page, not paginated. It is read in a dark theatre between dances, so a row carries only its running-order number and the judge's own score, and the filter is the only control above the list.
- The presentation the judge has open is kept in the URL, so returning to the tab or a revalidation reopens it. Nothing opens on a day that is not open or on a withdrawn presentation.
- After a score saves, the judge moves to the next presentation still owed, read from the revalidated list, and the form closes when none is left. A refusal keeps the form exactly as it was, since there is no offline copy.
- A sheet takes the whole page rather than a dialog, since it is longer than a dialog holds. It has no previous or next buttons; saving is the way on.
- The score dialog never closes on an outside tap, since in a dark theatre that tap is an accident. Every other exit asks first when there is a score to lose. The dialog is laid out like the sheet so the two read the same.
- The score field keeps digits and one point in the order typed: a second point is dropped, not moved. It shows its maximum after the value even when empty, since a sheet's lines have different maxima.
- An empty score field and an invalid one show the same message, since the judge meant the same score in either case.
- A sheet opens on the judge's own saved lines. An unsaved adding line is empty and a deducting line is 0, so a stray tap never stores a real 0 for a line the judge has not scored.
- The live totals count a half-typed value as nothing. Each part's sum is unclamped; only the sheet total is clamped to 0 to 100.
- A sheet is laid out the same way for the judge and for administration's correction: the `Devolución` first, then its parts with live sums and the total beneath them.
- The `Devolución` is one shared row. The take starts from a red dot, never a play icon, which becomes a pause once the take is live; the same row plays it back with its waveform and a delete for the judge.
- The microphone's permission, the recorder and the analyser failing are one refusal as far as the judge is concerned: the take did not start, and the judge sees the microphone message.
- A take still recording when the judge taps `Guardar` is finished and saved with the score, and a save waiting on the take is posted once.
- The waveform is a shape, not a measurement: 48 bars, with the mic level scaled so a normal voice fills them. Where the row is narrow, the oldest bars are cut, so a live take keeps showing the level it just read.
- A take's five-minute cap counts recorded time and leaves out pauses.

## `Gran final`

The `grandFinal` is the one ranking of academies in the domain. The public votes for an event's `finalist` academies in a `votingRound`, and the ranking is not an `award` (ADR-0018). Eligibility and picks are per modality, so one academy can be a finalist in one modality and not another.

### Finalist picks

- An academy's `Gran final` page exists once per modality, on its row of the modality's list, and only where the academy has a row: eligible there or picked there. An academy the list does not hold has no page there to read or save.
- Both of a finalist's banners are optional on save. The vote's opening needs every finalist to have both, and a refused opening names every blocker, including the finalists short of banners.
- Saving an academy's page checks its judges first, so a refused save uploads nothing. Then the judges and the banners are written in one transaction, and the picks are checked again under the write's lock. A save that takes the last judge off a modality the academy no longer qualifies in returns to the list, with its toast carried there.
- A pick outlives eligibility. An academy that stopped qualifying in a modality stays on that modality's list with its judges, who can only be taken off, and its name stays visible. A judge can be added only where the academy is still eligible; a removal is always allowed.
- Moving a judge's pick to another academy in the same modality takes it from that academy. The page asks first, naming each academy that will lose the judge, and the save names them before it writes.
- Only the event's judges pick. A judge must be a judge user with a presentation or a pick in the event, and every pick names one of them.
- A judge's own save checks, in order: the modality belongs to the active event, the judge is one of the event's judges, the judging window is open, and then eligibility is read again.
- On the judge's list, a saved pick whose academy stopped being eligible still reads as picked, and saving it again is refused by the server. Picks open under the same window as the scores.
- The portal home shows one `Gran final` alert, by precedence: a finalist of the open round gets the vote link to share; an eligible academy is told it meets the requirements; any other academy is invited only while some schedule still takes inscriptions, the only time it can still qualify. No alert names a modality, since eligibility is per modality.

### Voting rounds

- Closing a round closes the round its dialog was confirmed over, and no other. A close confirmed over round 1 after a `Desempate` opened finds round 1 closed and leaves round 2 open.
- A vote is cast only in the active event's open round, and only when it is the round the form was loaded in. A form loaded in round 1 and sent after a `Desempate` opened is refused, never cast in round 2.
- The tie rule for the round's first place is in ADR-0018's amendment.
- The admin result tab appears once a round closed. It ranks by weighted points with each finalist's share, calls out a round 1 tie for first place with what settles it, and calls out a `Desempate` still tied as a shared win. Nothing in the administration reveals totals while a round is open; an audit link does (§ Audit links).
- A vote that did not count is explained in a fixed order the voter can act on: a closed round first, then the identity, then the academy.

### Voter identity

- A visitor's identity on the public page takes precedence in this order: a code in the address, which votes with it and says nothing of a sign-in; then the voter's cookie; then the sign-in prompt.
- A signed voter cookie can outlive its voter row. Then it identifies nobody, and the page asks for a sign-in as it does with no cookie.
- The Google sign-in asks for an account choice each time, so a shared phone picks its account rather than reusing the last one.
- Google refuses sign-in from an app's built-in browser. There the page offers the sign-in only as a way out, and the ticket's QR code still votes from the camera's browser.
- The public privacy policy describes what the system keeps, so a change to a voter's stored fields must change it too. The Google consent screen links to it.

### Vote page

- Finalists show their pictures with a button each that chooses it, and the choice is confirmed from a bar fixed at the bottom. Only a visitor who can vote reaches it: a live code or a signed-in voter.
- A finalist's two banners autoplay in a 16:9 carousel every three seconds, until a swipe or a click stops it for good, so a person looking at one picture is not pulled to the next. The dots only say that a second picture exists.

### Vote codes

- A batch takes at most 1000 codes. A print run is a night's tickets at most; a larger one is several batches, each voidable alone.
- A `voteCode` is 128 random bits in base64url (22 characters, which a QR holds easily), so nobody can guess one or walk from one printed code to the next.
- Codes print as SVG QR codes at error-correction level M with a one-module margin, as the comprobante's QR does.
- The printed sheet names only the `Gran final` and `Escaneá y votá` on each code. The batch's name appears only in the toolbar, which does not print.
- Voiding a batch is final. A voided batch does not print, but a vote already cast with one of its codes still counts and reads as cast. The sheet's QR encodes the vote page on the app's configured origin, falling back to the origin of the request.

### Audit links

- An audit link is spent by the round it audits: the round open when it was issued, or else the next one to open, or the event's last round. A link is spent once a round of its event has closed after it was issued, so a link issued between round 1's close and a `Desempate` survives.
- The audit page counts its round's totals from the votes table, not from a counter, with the same tally as the published result. The open round's figures are read in one snapshot, so the cross-checks never disagree with the tally.
- Every audit request reads the link and its round again, so a revocation or a close takes effect on the next reload. Only the open round's figures come from the in-process cache.
- The audit token travels in the URL fragment, which browsers never send, so it stays out of server logs, `Referer` headers and chat link previews. The page sends it once, in the body of the form that opens the link, and the link opens only when the auditor presses the button.
- A link in the address wins over an earlier opening in the same browser. A refused link says why, and a round not yet open shows no figures.
- The audit page's browser session lasts 24 hours and covers the whole origin, since the page's data is read at a `.data` path that a cookie scoped to the audit path never reaches. Once a link is revoked, the next load drops its token, so every browser is refused.
- At most three audit links are live at once, where live means neither revoked nor spent. A creation past that is refused, the menu says why, and the event row is locked for the count.
- Creating an audit link hands it over in the same dialog, with its QR to scan from the screen and its address to copy. The auditor's name on the list opens it again later.
